Description

A ribbed beanie with a fold-up brim. It is worked flat in one piece, from side to side, then seamed once down one side and gathered closed at the top.

The ribbing comes from working into the back loop of each stitch. There is no special rib stitch to learn.

Skill level

Beginner. You need to chain, single crochet, slip stitch, and sew a gathered seam by hand.

Size

Adult medium. One size.

  • To fit a head circumference of 22 inches, 56 centimetres.
  • Finished height: 12 inches, 30.5 centimetres. This measurement already includes the fold, so do not add extra.
  • Finished flat length before seaming: approximately 20 inches, 51 centimetres.

The fabric is very stretchy, which is why the flat length is about 2 inches, 5 centimetres, smaller than the head it fits.

Materials

  • Acrylic yarn, 100 percent, weight 4 medium. This weight is also sold as worsted, afghan, or aran.
  • Approximately 100 to 120 grams of that yarn, about 180 to 220 metres. One 100 gram skein is likely to fall just short, so buy two skeins, or one 150 gram skein.
  • Crochet hook, 5 millimetres. This size is also marked H-8.
  • Measuring tape.
  • Scissors.
  • Darning needle, or sewing needle.
  • Stitch markers, or safety pins.

Stitch counts and measurements

Every row in this hat has 40 stitches. The hat is 66 rows long in total.

  • The starting chain of 41 chains measures approximately 12 inches, 30.5 centimetres.
  • 40 stitches across a row measures approximately 12 inches, 30.5 centimetres.
  • 66 rows measures approximately 20 inches, 51 centimetres.

If your stitches come out tighter or looser than these measurements, work to the counts rather than to the measurements. The hat will still fit, because the fabric stretches.

If the fabric feels stiff and will not stretch, use a larger hook. If you can feel gaps between the stitches, use a smaller hook.

Counting 66 rows is easier if you mark your place as you go. Move a stitch marker to the first stitch of every tenth row, or use a row counter.

Working into the back loop

Every stitch has two loops across its top: one nearer to you, and one further away. The one further away is the back loop.

To work into the back loop only, insert the hook under that further loop alone, and leave the nearer loop untouched. The untouched loops build up into raised ridges, and those ridges are the ribbing.

Row 1 is worked into the starting chain in the ordinary way, under both loops. Every row after that is worked into the back loop only.

Instructions

Foundation chain

  1. Make a slip knot on the hook.
  2. Chain 41. One of these 41 chains becomes the turning chain, so this gives 40 stitches in the first row.

Row 1

  1. Skip the chain nearest the hook.
  2. Work 1 single crochet into the next chain, going under both loops of that chain in the ordinary way.
  3. Place a stitch marker or safety pin into this first stitch.
  4. Work 1 single crochet into each remaining chain to the end of the row, still under both loops.

You now have 40 stitches.

Row 2

  1. Chain 1, then turn your work.
  2. Work 1 single crochet into the back loop only of each stitch across the row.
  3. Place a stitch marker into the first stitch of this row.

You have 40 stitches.

Marking the first stitch of each row, at both edges of the work, is what stops you from losing the last stitch of a row. Move both markers up as you go.

Rows 3 to 66

Repeat Row 2 another 64 times, so that you have worked 66 rows in total, each of 40 stitches.

The piece should now measure approximately 20 inches, 51 centimetres, along its long edge, with the fabric relaxed and not stretched.

End after a completed row. Do not cut the yarn yet.

Seam

  1. Fold the piece in half so that the two short ends meet, and line them up stitch for stitch. The long edges are now the bottom and the top of the hat.
  2. Working through both layers together, slip stitch the two short ends together, all the way from one long edge to the other.
  3. Turn the hat inside out, so that the seam sits on the inside.
  4. Cut the yarn, leaving a tail of about 20 inches, 51 centimetres. Draw the tail through the last loop on the hook and pull it tight to secure it.

Gather the top

  1. Choose which long edge is the top of the hat. Either edge works, because both are the same.
  2. Thread the 20 inch tail onto the darning needle.
  3. Take running stitches all the way around that top edge: in through the fabric, out through the fabric, keeping the stitches evenly spaced and close to the edge.
  4. When you have gone all the way around, pull the tail firmly. The top will draw together and close.
  5. Knot the tail to hold it.

Finishing

  1. Use the remaining tail to close the small hole left at the centre of the gathered top.
  2. Weave in all ends and trim them.
  3. Fold the bottom edge of the hat up to the depth you like.

A note on crochet terms

This pattern uses United States crochet terms. In United Kingdom terms, the stitch called single crochet here is called double crochet.
