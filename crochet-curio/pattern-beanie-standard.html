Foldable Ribbed Beanie, Adult Medium

A crochet pattern by Crochet Curio. Version 1.0. Published 29 August 2026.
Standard edition. A large print edition, written out in full with no abbreviations, is also available.

Description

A ribbed beanie with a fold-up brim, worked flat in one piece from side to side, then seamed once down one side and gathered closed at the top. The ribbing comes from working into the back loop; there is no special rib stitch to learn.

Skill level: beginner. Chain, single crochet, slip stitch, and a gathered top closed with a needle.

Size

Adult medium, one size. To fit a head circumference of 22 in (56 cm).

  • Finished height: 12 in (30.5 cm), fold included. Do not add extra.
  • Finished circumference, unstretched: 20 in (51 cm).

The fabric is very stretchy, which is why the finished circumference is about 2 in (5 cm) smaller than the head it fits.

Materials

  • Yarn: 100% acrylic, weight 4 medium (worsted, afghan, aran). 100–120 g, approx 180–220 m. One 100 g skein is likely to fall just short. Buy two, or one 150 g skein.
  • Hook: 5 mm (H-8).
  • Notions: darning needle, stitch markers or safety pins, measuring tape, scissors.

Special stitches

Single crochet in back loop only. Each stitch has two loops across its top: one nearer you, one further away. Insert the hook under the further loop alone and leave the nearer loop untouched, then complete the single crochet as usual. The untouched loops build into the raised ridges that make the ribbing.

Row 1 is worked into the foundation chain under both loops in the ordinary way. Every row after that is single crochet in back loop only.

Pattern

Worked flat, side to side, in one piece. Every row is 40 stitches; the count never changes.

Foundation
Chain 41.
Row 1
Single crochet in 2nd chain from hook and in each chain across, under both loops. (40 single crochet)
Row 2
Chain 1, turn. Single crochet in back loop only in each stitch across. (40 single crochet)
Rows 3–66
Repeat Row 2. (40 single crochet)

Do not fasten off. Piece measures approx 20 in (51 cm) along the long edge, relaxed.

Tip: mark the first stitch of every 10th row, or use a row counter. Marking the first stitch of each row at both edges is also what stops you losing the last stitch of a row.

Image 1. The flat piece. 66 rows wide, 40 stitches tall; ribbing runs the short way.

Assembly

  1. Fold the piece in half so the two short ends meet, and line them up stitch for stitch. The long edges are now the bottom and top of the hat.
  2. Working through both layers, slip stitch the two short ends together from one long edge to the other.
  3. Turn the hat inside out so the seam sits inside.
  4. Cut the yarn leaving a 20 in (51 cm) tail. Draw it through the last loop and pull tight.

Image 2. Seamed into a tube. Short ends joined into an open tube; the seam tail hangs at the top.

Gather the top

  1. Choose either long edge as the top: both are the same.
  2. Thread the 20 in tail on the darning needle.
  3. Work running stitches all the way around the top edge, evenly spaced and close to the edge.
  4. Pull the tail firmly to draw the top closed. Knot to hold.

Image 3. Running stitch around the top edge. Needle running a stitch around the top edge, ready to draw closed.

Finishing

  1. Use the remaining tail to close the small hole at the centre of the gathered top.
  2. Weave in all ends and trim.
  3. Fold the bottom edge up to the depth you like.

Image 4. The gathered top. Top pulled shut into gathered pleats meeting at the centre. The excess yarn tail is used to sew the top opening closed with a darning needle, and the end is woven in.

Image 5. The finished hat. The finished beanie, brim turned up.
