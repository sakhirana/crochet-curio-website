Foldable Ribbed Beanie, Adult Medium

A crochet pattern by Crochet Curio. Version 1.0. Published 29 August 2026.
Standard edition. A large print edition, written out in full with no abbreviations, is also available.

Description

A ribbed beanie with a fold-up brim, worked flat in one piece from side to side, then seamed once down one side and gathered closed at the top. The ribbing comes from working into the back loop; there is no special rib stitch to learn.

Skill level: beginner. Chain, single crochet, slip stitch, and one hand-sewn gathered seam.

Size

Adult medium, one size. To fit a head circumference of 22 in (56 cm).

  • Finished height: 12 in (30.5 cm), fold included — do not add extra.
  • Finished circumference, unstretched: 20 in (51 cm).

The fabric is very stretchy, which is why the finished circumference is about 2 in (5 cm) smaller than the head it fits.

Materials

  • Yarn: 100% acrylic, weight 4 medium (worsted, afghan, aran). 100–120 g, approx 180–220 m. One 100 g skein is likely to fall just short — buy two, or one 150 g skein.
  • Hook: 5 mm (H-8).
  • Notions: darning needle, stitch markers or safety pins, measuring tape, scissors.

Gauge

13 sts and 13 rows = 4 in (10 cm) in sc blo, measured flat and unstretched.

Gauge is not critical for fit, because the rib stretches. It is worth checking anyway: if the fabric feels stiff and will not stretch, size up your hook; if you can see gaps between the stitches, size down. If your gauge differs, work to the stitch and row counts rather than to the measurements — the hat will still fit.

Special stitches

sc blo — single crochet in back loop only. Each stitch has two loops across its top: one nearer you, one further away. Insert the hook under the further loop alone and leave the nearer loop untouched, then complete the sc as usual. The untouched loops build into the raised ridges that make the ribbing.

Row 1 is worked into the foundation chain under both loops in the ordinary way. Every row after that is sc blo.

Pattern

Worked flat, side to side, in one piece. Every row is 40 sts; the count never changes.

Foundation
Ch 41.
Row 1
Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across, under both loops. (40 sc)
Row 2
Ch 1, turn. Sc blo in each st across. (40 sc)
Rows 3–66
Rep Row 2. (40 sc)

Do not fasten off. Piece measures approx 20 in (51 cm) along the long edge, relaxed.

Tip: mark the first st of every 10th row, or use a row counter. Marking the first stitch of each row at both edges is also what stops you losing the last stitch of a row.

Figure 1. The flat piece. A rectangle, wider than it is tall. The two long edges run left to right: the upper becomes the top of the hat and is gathered closed, the lower becomes the brim. The two short edges, drawn heavier, are sewn together to make the seam. Thin lines run from one long edge to the other: these are the back loop ridges, and they run brim to crown on the finished hat, never around it. The rectangle is 66 rows from side to side, about 20 in (51 cm), and 40 sts from bottom to top, about 12 in (30.5 cm).

Assembly

  1. Fold the piece in half so the two short ends meet, and line them up st for st. The long edges are now the bottom and top of the hat.
  2. Working through both layers, sl st the two short ends together from one long edge to the other.
  3. Turn the hat inside out so the seam sits inside.
  4. Cut the yarn leaving a 20 in (51 cm) tail. Draw it through the last loop and pull tight.

Figure 2. Seamed into a tube. The same piece with its two short ends brought together and sewn, making an open tube. The seam is a single vertical line down one side, drawn heavier than the rest. Both ends are open at this stage: the top edge is still a full circle, the bottom edge is the brim. The ribbing now runs from bottom to top all the way around. The tube is turned inside out after seaming, so the seam sits inside.

Gather the top

  1. Choose either long edge as the top — both are the same.
  2. Thread the 20 in tail on the darning needle.
  3. Work running sts all the way around the top edge, evenly spaced and close to the edge.
  4. Pull the tail firmly to draw the top closed. Knot to hold.

Finishing

  1. Use the remaining tail to close the small hole at the centre of the gathered top.
  2. Weave in all ends and trim.
  3. Fold the bottom edge up to the depth you like.

Figure 3. The finished hat. The hat as worn. The top edge has been drawn closed with a running stitch and knotted, so the crown gathers to a small point. The body hangs straight down from the crown. At the bottom, a deeper band shows the brim turned up against the outside. The ribbing runs up and down throughout, on the brim as well as the body, because the brim is the same fabric folded. Finished height is about 12 in (30.5 cm) with the brim already folded.
